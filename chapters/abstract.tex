The growing sophistication of cyber threats poses critical challenges to network security, making machine learning-based Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) an essential strategy for autonomously identifying attack patterns. In this context, this work proposes an experimental laboratory comparing \textit{Random Forest} and Deep Neural Network (DNN) as supervised classifiers, evaluating the effects of data quality and dimensionality reduction via \textit{Mean Decrease in Impurity} (MDI) on the CICIDS2017 and LycoS-IDS2017 datasets. As an unsupervised extension, an \textit{Autoencoder} trained exclusively on benign traffic was investigated as an alternative for unknown attack detection. \textit{Random Forest} outperformed DNN in all evaluated scenarios, reaching a Macro F1 of 0.97 on LycoS-IDS2017 versus 0.87 for DNN, with inference time up to 5.18 times faster; the DNN showed difficulties with minority classes and greater sensitivity to data quality, gaining 0.20 Macro F1 points when replacing CICIDS2017 with LycoS-IDS2017, compared to 0.11 for \textit{Random Forest}. MDI-based dimensionality reduction degraded all models by favoring majority-class features at the expense of discriminative characteristics for rare attacks. The \textit{Autoencoder} achieved a Macro F1 of 0.89 and an attack Recall of 0.82 on LycoS-IDS2017, demonstrating partial viability for unlabeled scenarios, though without surpassing supervised classifiers on known attacks. It is concluded that \textit{Random Forest} with curated data is the most suitable solution for environments with known attacks, that dataset quality proves more decisive than architectural complexity, and that unknown anomaly detection remains an open challenge.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Intrusion detection. \textit{Random Forest}. Deep neural networks. \textit{Autoencoder}. CICIDS2017.
